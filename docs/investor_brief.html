🌱MOWR

Investor brief · pre-launch

Lawn care, on demand — becoming the logistics network for recurring home services.

Draw your lawn, get an instant price, and book a vetted local mower — pay only when it's done. Then it repeats on its own: recurring jobs, allocated to the nearest available mower the night before, with a live ETA. Like food delivery, for the outside of the home.

Product built Single Flutter app · 3 roles In-house routing engine United Kingdom
01The problem

Booking a mow is still stuck in the classifieds era.

Every other everyday service has been made instant — a car, a takeaway, a food shop arrive in a few taps with a price and an ETA. Lawn care hasn't moved. Homeowners still chase quotes, wait days for a callback, haggle, get no-shows, and pay in cash. Mowers, meanwhile, waste half their day driving inefficient routes and chasing invoices.

📮

For homeowners

Opaque pricing, slow quotes, unvetted strangers, no tracking, cash-in-hand.

🛻

For mowers

Feast-or-famine work, dead miles between jobs, and payment they have to chase.

🔁

The gap

Lawns need doing every 1–4 weeks, forever. That natural recurrence is pure recurring revenue nobody has productised.

02The solution

A price in seconds, a vetted mower, paid when it's done.

MOWR is a three-sided marketplace in one app — customer, mower, and admin — on one backend. The customer flow is live and built today:

Draw your lawn, see your price Built

Trace the lawn on satellite imagery; the app measures the exact area and perimeter geodesically and prices it instantly — mowing, edging, grass length. No quote, no wait. Real UK address + rooftop-level location lookup.

Book a vetted local mower Built

Mowers are admin-approved, identity-anchored (phone-verified, KYC'd through payouts), and — on the roadmap — background-checked. The customer pays only after the job is completed.

On-site honesty, not disputes Built

If a lawn is bigger than measured, the mower re-measures on site; the price recomputes server-side and — over a threshold — the customer approves before a penny moves.

Mowers get paid automatically Built

Stripe Connect handles payouts and their KYC; MOWR takes a per-mower commission. Mowers see live earnings, route, and one-tap navigation to each job.

The full screen-by-screen build is documented in the live Rendered Screens reference.

04Where this goes

The recurring engine: subscriptions, auto-allocated the night before, with an ETA.

A one-off mow is a transaction. A lawn mowed every fortnight, forever, is a subscription — and the logistics of fulfilling thousands of them efficiently is the real business. This is how MOWR scales, and the foundation is already in the app.

STEP 1

Customer sets a rhythm

"Every 2 weeks." Booked once; it recurs on its own. Built.

STEP 2

The night before

An optimiser gathers the next day's due jobs and every opted-in mower, and allocates each job to the mower who can do it most efficiently. Roadmap — engine built.

STEP 3

Routed & sequenced

Each mower gets an optimised day: least driving, honouring every time window. Built (tested).

STEP 4

Customer gets an ETA

"Your mower arrives ~10:40am." A tracked, delivery-grade experience for the outside of the home. Roadmap.

The route optimiser that powers step 3 is written and unit-tested today. The fleet allocator in step 2 is the same engine applied across every mower — score each job against each mower, assign the best. Kept deliberately in-house: routing is the operational heart of a scaled home-services network, and owning it protects the margin forever.

06Market · illustrative

Millions of UK lawns, cut on a schedule, paid in cash today.

The UK has roughly 23 million households, a large share with a private garden and lawn. Lawn maintenance is a routine, recurring spend currently served by a long tail of sole-trader gardeners and cash jobs — fragmented, offline, and ripe for aggregation.

07Business model · illustrative unit economics

A clean take rate on a repeating basket.

MOWR keeps a commission on every job (15% default, tunable per mower). The magic is recurrence: acquire a customer once, earn on every mow for years. An illustrative mid-size lawn on a fortnightly plan:

Illustrative — one recurring customerPer mowPer year (≈22 mows)
Customer pays (200 m² lawn + edging)£54.00£1,188
Mower payout (85%)£45.90£1,010
MOWR gross (15% take)£8.10£178

Recurring > one-off

One acquisition, years of margin. A loyalty engine ("3rd mow 20% off") is built to drive the switch from one-off to subscription.

Density lifts it

Tighter routes mean more jobs per mower-hour — raising mower pay and MOWR's absolute margin per route without raising prices.

More per address

Each new service (edging today; hedges, gutters next) stacks revenue on customers already acquired.

Example only, using MOWR's real pricing structure and commission. Not a forecast or current result.
